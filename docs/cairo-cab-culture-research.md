## 1. How people hail cabs in Cairo

**Observed.** Street-hailing in Cairo is destination-first: state the destination before getting in, and agree the total fare before boarding if the taxi has no working meter. ([Wikipedia — Taxis by country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxis_by_country)) A driver may refuse the trip if the destination is inconvenient, too far, or the fare too low; the standard advice is to decline and try the next cab rather than argue. ([Wikipedia — Taxis by country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxis_by_country)) Reported hailing phrases: "[destination], kam?" ("how much?"), "Bel-meter, law samaht" ("by the meter, please"), and "La, shukran" to decline. ([Cairo Airport portal taxi guide](https://www.cairo-airport.com/))

**Observed.** The audition runs both ways: the driver asks where you are going before opening the door, because accepting a fare is a decision about his own route and time. ([Wikipedia — Taxis by country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxis_by_country)) Local press reviewing Cairo's first taxi apps in December 2014 opened with "Hailing a taxi in Cairo can be a bit of a gamble. Most of the time, it's perfectly fine." ([Mada Masr — Trying 2 taxi apps in Cairo, Dec 10, 2014](https://madamasr.com/en/2016/02/04/feature/politics/taxi-drivers-protest-against-uber-other-app-based-car-services/))

**Observed.** Older black-and-white cabs generally had no working meter, so the total fare for all passengers was agreed before boarding; white cabs are the metered reform generation, but meters remain distrusted, so pre-boarding agreement survives even in metered cabs. ([Cairo Airport portal taxi guide](https://www.cairo-airport.com/)) ([CairoScene — Why Egypt's Taxis Are Different Colours](https://cairoscene.com/Society/Why-Egypt-s-Taxis-Are-Different-Colours-in-Every-City))

**Observed.** Commonly repeated customs: agree the fare before entering or insist on the meter because drivers may claim the meter is broken or quote an inflated price; count change before leaving because drivers may claim a smaller note was handed over or return the wrong amount. ([Egypt Travel Blog](https://egypttravelblog.com/)) ([The Travelling Squid — A Rigged Meter on a Cairo Cab](https://www.thetravellingsquid.com/))

**Observed (industry confirmation of the meter problem).** In January 2020 the head of Egypt's White Taxi Association, opposing Uber's white-cab program, said "Uber used to constantly attack the service provided by white taxis... claiming that the drivers intentionally deactivate the taximeters." The meter-off habit was real enough to become the centerpiece of the opposing party's accusation. ([Egypt Independent, Jan 29, 2020](https://www.egyptindependent.com/uber-egypt-announces-plan-for-cab-hailing-service-white-taxi-association-voices-opposition/))

**Observed.** Seating and hiring norms: women commonly sit in the back seat, especially alone with a male driver, as a safety and social preference. ([UK FCDO — Egypt travel advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/egypt/safety-and-security)) Street cabs are normally hired whole-ride (fare for the trip, not per person); per-seat shared riding belongs to microbuses on known routes. ([Wikipedia — Share taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_taxi))

**Observed (literary evidence).** Khaled Al Khamissi's *Taxi* (2007) collects 58 fictional monologues by Cairo taxi drivers on bootleg video games, Nile fishing, malnourished policemen, Kurosawa's influence on Egyptian cinema, veiled women's double lives, censorship, repression, and the wars. ([Wikipedia — Khaled Al Khamissi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Al_Khamissi)) ([Guardian review, Nov 1, 2011](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2011/nov/01/taxi-khaled-alkhamissi-review))

**Unverified folklore — do not ship as fact.** The following could not be sourced this pass: "tizkar" / تزكار as the standard term for shouting a destination; "the meter is a foreigner's meter" as a fixed saying; "bill ma'ak?" as a named change trick; banging the ceiling or door as a standard stop signal (one travel source explicitly doubts it); routine mid-route pickup of strangers in private cabs; taxi-stand queue place-protection; "do you work the Pyramid road?" as a fixed formula; and the "fi rihab Allah" (in God's care) free-ride custom. All are plausible texture for fiction, none may be presented as fact.

## 3. How cabs changed color and what colors mean

**Observed.** Taxi licensing is governed at the governorate level; each local authority registers and monitors its own fleet and chose its own color scheme. "Distinct colour schemes made enforcement easier long before GPS tracking or centralised databases. So, if a taxi licensed in Matrouh began operating routinely in Cairo, it was immediately identifiable. Colour functioned as a visual boundary." ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Society/Why-Egypt-s-Taxis-Are-Different-Colours-in-Every-City))

**Observed (verified palette, all from CairoScene):**

| Governorate | Scheme |
| --- | --- |
| Cairo | black and white (traditional); newer metered generation solid white, often with black checkered stripe |
| Alexandria | yellow and black (the yellow-and-black Lada is the on-screen shorthand for Alexandria) |
| Damanhour | green and white |
| Sharqia | red and white OR burgundy and white — both official, running simultaneously |
| Beheira | white and green |
| Monofeya | green and yellow |
| Kafr El-Sheikh | yellow and white |
| Marsa Matrouh | white and sky blue |
| Ismailia | orange and white |
| Sohag | navy and white |
| Qena | purple and white |
| Red Sea | orange and black |

The same logic is international: Mexico City's pink-and-white, Morocco's per-city petit taxis (red Casablanca, blue Tangier, beige Marrakech), China's municipal fleets — "decentralised governance produces decentralised fleets." ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Society/Why-Egypt-s-Taxis-Are-Different-Colours-in-Every-City))

**Observed.** Pink (2015) is the first Egyptian taxi color chosen for social meaning rather than administration: it appeared after a UN study reported that more than 99% of women in Egypt had experienced sexual harassment, on the premise that women driving women in fully pink cars would create safety. ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Society/Why-Egypt-s-Taxis-Are-Different-Colours-in-Every-City))

**Observed.** The reform was explicitly anti-negotiation and explicitly unsuccessful: "The new taxis are solid white, air-conditioned, and metered — a reform, the government said. A break from bargaining and chaos. ... But you still hail them, negotiate before the door is closed, and submit whatever is next." ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Society/Why-Egypt-s-Taxis-Are-Different-Colours-in-Every-City))

**Observed.** The app era ended color-reading: "You look at the screen, watch as the little car moves toward your dot. You do not look for colour any more, you scan car plates for letters and numbers." ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Society/Why-Egypt-s-Taxis-Are-Different-Colours-in-Every-City))

## 4. Destination negotiation and demand

**Observed.** The reverse-selection dynamic is verified: drivers refuse inconvenient, far, or low-fare trips, so at peak friction the driver chooses the passenger. ([Wikipedia — Taxis by country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxis_by_country)) Fare-by-destination before boarding is the norm wherever meters are absent or deactivated. ([Wikipedia — Taxis by country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxis_by_country)) ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Society/Why-Egypt-s-Taxis-Are-Different-Colours-in-Every-City))

**Observed (pricing data point).** When Uber proposed putting white cabs on its app, the White Taxi Association's counter-analysis priced the app white-cab fare at LE7.25 base plus LE2.5/km (LE3/km past 30 km) — cheaper than Uber cars but still dearer than a usual negotiated white-cab trip. ([Egypt Independent, Jan 29, 2020](https://www.egyptindependent.com/uber-egypt-announces-plan-for-cab-hailing-service-white-taxi-association-voices-opposition/))

**Observed.** Fare disputes follow the ride in the app era, including the payment method itself: a 2024 case turned on a driver and passenger who "simply disputed over the value of the fare... as he refused to pay according to the 'Visa' system." ([Egypt Independent, May 16, 2024](https://www.egyptindependent.com/court-sets-date-to-consider-lawsuit-against-uber-and-careem-to-revoke-their-licenses-in-egypt/))

**Unverified this pass.** Explicit route-matching refusal criteria ("going my way"), time-of-day refusal patterns, and neighborhood refusal tables. The verified refusal-plus-quote dynamic is enough for the mechanic below.

## 5. The Uber-vs-taxi conflict in Egypt (and the Paris mix-up)

**Observed (2016 street conflict).** Wikipedia's Egypt section states there were "several protests, sit-ins, and violent attacks by taxi operators in 2016," citing Mada Masr (Feb 4, 2016) and Ahram Online (Mar 8, 2016, open-ended sit-in demanding Uber and Careem shutdown). ([Wikipedia Egypt section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legality_of_ridesharing_companies_by_jurisdiction)) AP directly verifies the March 8, 2016 action: drivers blocked a major road, witnesses reported one tear-gas canister fired to disperse them, drivers complained Uber drivers were "not subject to the same taxes or licensing fees," and Cairo's head of traffic police declared car-hailing apps illegal as drivers must hold a special license to pick up clients, "adding they are clamping down on them." ([AP News, Mar 8, 2016](https://apnews.com/general-news-ff96b31802244e77ae51b657df2b9d2e)) The grievance, per Asharq Al-Awsat: app drivers "were not required to pay the hefty fees to operate transportation vehicles." ([Asharq Al-Awsat, Mar 20, 2018](https://english.aawsat.com/home/article/1211186/egypt-halts-operation-uber-careem-ride-sharing-apps))

**Observed (court battle).** March 20, 2018: the Administrative Court orders the government to shut Uber and Careem down and close their apps for "using normal cars for commercial use without a license"; both companies announce continuation and appeal the next day. ([Egypt Independent, May 8, 2018](https://www.egyptindependent.com/parliament-passes-law-regulating-uber-careem-work/)) The case was filed with lawyer Khaled El Gamal: "Amid several protests from the taxi drivers with no official response, those drivers decided to direct their issue to the court." ([Arab News, Apr 4, 2018](https://www.arabnews.com/business/uber-careem-stuck-in-cairo-legal-gridlock-1278661)) April 2018: the pair fight on two fronts — a draft law requiring servers inside Egypt, data links to government, jail powers for drivers without documents, and a ban on government employees driving (many public-sector workers top up salaries with ride-hail work). ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/business/uber-careem-stuck-in-cairo-legal-gridlock-1278661)) May 7, 2018: parliament legalizes ride-hailing with LE30M-per-company fees over three years and government data access; the State Council had objected that earlier drafts violated constitutional privacy guarantees. ([Egypt Independent, May 8, 2018](https://www.egyptindependent.com/parliament-passes-law-regulating-uber-careem-work/)) Search-synthesized: a Cairo court suspended enforcement of the ban in early April 2018, and the Supreme Administrative Court definitively lifted it on Feb 23, 2019.

**Observed (2020 detente attempt).** Uber proposed incorporating Egypt's white cabs into its service, calling them "an integral part" of public transport; the White Taxi Association refused "in the strongest of terms," citing the meter-deactivation accusations and the 15% + 2.1% commission, and announced its own GPS-tracked app "Smart B" with fares within LE2 of street prices. Careem had tried including white cabs about two years earlier and abandoned the attempt. ([Egypt Independent, Jan 29, 2020](https://www.egyptindependent.com/uber-egypt-announces-plan-for-cab-hailing-service-white-taxi-association-voices-opposition/))

**Observed (2024 revival).** After the March 14, 2024 death of Habiba Shamaa — she jumped from a moving Uber at 100 km/h during an attempted kidnapping — and further incidents, the Administrative Court scheduled the first hearing of a lawsuit to revoke Uber/Careem licenses, citing Law 87/2018 Article 8 and demanding in-car surveillance linked to the Interior Ministry. ([Egypt Independent, May 16, 2024](https://www.egyptindependent.com/court-sets-date-to-consider-lawsuit-against-uber-and-careem-to-revoke-their-licenses-in-egypt/))

**Correction to the "beat up by taxi driver gangs" memory.** The famous footage of cabbies burning cars and attacking Uber drivers is Paris, France: Jan 13, 2014 (car attacked near Charles de Gaulle) and the June 25, 2015 Paris lockdown where cabbies burned cars and attacked Uber drivers (including the Courtney Love hostage incident). ([Wikipedia France section, citing TechCrunch and The Verge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legality_of_ridesharing_companies_by_jurisdiction)) The verified Egyptian record is protests, sit-ins, road blockades, tear gas, lawsuits, and Wikipedia's summary of "violent attacks by taxi operators in 2016" — but this pass found no single sourced incident of a mob beating an Uber driver in Cairo. If the game needs street violence against app drivers, either ground it in the French precedent or keep it fictional; do not attribute the Paris imagery to Cairo.

## 7. Mechanics translation — design inference for PAIN TAXI

Each item names the real finding it translates. All are **Inferred** design ideas; verified findings are cited. Language follows CONTEXT.md (Fare, Shift, Cash Quota, Rival, Pit Stop) and the GDD systems (pickup zones, customer Distance/Wealth/Max-Acceptable-Damage, Bail-out System).

1. **Reverse-hailing auditions.** Finding: the driver interviews the passenger by destination before the door closes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxis_by_country)). Mechanic: pickup zones show destination + payout first; Fares audition the player — each passenger has a hidden trip-direction preference and refuses cabs whose heading doesn't suit the trip. Inverts the Crazy Taxi loop: the passenger picks the cab. Slots into the existing customer-selection system (customers already prefer the least-damaged vehicle). Rank: most distinctive.
2. **Quote-based haggling where route match beats distance.** Finding: fare fixed by destination before boarding; meters distrusted ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Society/Why-Egypt-s-Taxis-Are-Different-Colours-in-Every-City)). Mechanic: payout is quoted at pickup and weighted by whether the destination lies along the player's current heading; the meter ticks decoratively during the ride and does not determine pay. Rank: very distinctive.
3. **Detour re-haggle.** Finding: "negotiated before the door closed, submitted to whatever came next... the detour only he knew" ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Society/Why-Egypt-s-Taxis-Are-Different-Colours-in-Every-City)). Mechanic: taking a shortcut mid-Fare triggers a renegotiation minigame; success pays a bonus, failure accelerates the passenger's Bail-out timer. Rank: very distinctive.
4. **Livery tiers as rule sets.** Finding: per-governorate liveries, the white metered reform, the 2015 pink fleet ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Society/Why-Egypt-s-Taxis-Are-Different-Colours-in-Every-City)). Mechanic: repaint between Shifts at the Pit Stop to enter different districts under different rules — black-and-white (street-negotiation rules: richer quotes, no app fares), white checker (app fares, fixed lower payouts), pink (safety-bonus fares, exclusive passenger pool). Ship as fictional flavor, not the real decree scheme. Rank: very distinctive.
5. **Meter integrity as an app-fare gate.** Finding: the industry accusation that drivers "intentionally deactivate the taximeters" ([Egypt Independent, Jan 2020](https://www.egyptindependent.com/uber-egypt-announces-plan-for-cab-hailing-service-white-taxi-association-voices-opposition/)). Mechanic: app fares require an intact meter; combat damage breaks eligibility, forcing a choice between street-negotiation payouts and app payouts. Rank: distinctive.
6. **Blockade hazards.** Finding: taxi drivers physically blocked roads in protest and security responded with tear gas ([AP, Mar 2016](https://apnews.com/general-news-ff96b31802244e77ae51b657df2b9d2e)); bridge blockades predate Uber (6th October bridge, Mar 25, 2013 file photo caption in [Ahram coverage](https://english.ahram.org.eg/NewsAFCON2025/2025/187252.aspx)). Mechanic: union zones occasionally spawn burning-tire roadblocks that reroute the optimal line; Rivals affiliated with the union can open them. Ship as fiction inspired by the record, not a named-union reenactment. Rank: distinctive.
7. **App-vs-street double mode.** Finding: 90,000 ride-hail drivers in Cairo whose cars are indistinguishable from private cars; "you do not look for colour any more, you scan car plates" ([CairoScene](https://cairoscene.com/Society/Why-Egypt-s-Taxis-Are-Different-Colours-in-Every-City)). Mechanic: app mode pays flat algorithmic fares but marks the player as a target inside street-driver hostility zones; street mode pays negotiated quotes but spawns app drivers sniping your pickup zones. Rank: distinctive.
8. **The change trick.** Finding: count change before leaving; wrong-change and small-note claims are the classic dispute ([Egypt Travel Blog](https://egypttravelblog.com/)). Mechanic: payout minigame where the player catches a wrong-note return; success keeps the margin, failure donates it to a Rival. Rank: distinctive.
9. **Fare-sharing risk/reward.** Finding: private cabs are whole-ride hire; per-seat riding belongs to microbuses ([Wikipedia — Share taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_taxi)). Mechanic: optional second pickup en route pays bonus cash but risks the first passenger bailing. Rank: distinctive.
10. **Cash-vs-card fare disputes.** Finding: fare value disputes over the payment method itself ([Egypt Independent, May 2024](https://www.egyptindependent.com/court-sets-date-to-consider-lawsuit-against-uber-and-careem-to-revoke-their-licenses-in-egypt/)); Uber added cash payments in Dec 2015 ([Mada Masr](https://madamasr.com/en/2016/02/04/feature/politics/taxi-drivers-protest-against-uber-other-app-based-car-services/)). Mechanic: some Fares pay only in cash tokens, others only in card; juggling both adds routing pressure. Rank: middling.
11. **Charity-ride karma.** Finding: "fi rihab Allah" custom unverified. Mechanic if used: occasional no-fare passenger whose kindness pays out later; label pure fiction. Rank: sweet, currently invented.
12. **Ceiling-knock stop signal.** Finding: unverified; one source doubts it. Mechanic if used: knock audio cue for a fast drop-off bonus; label pure fiction. Rank: middling and risky.
